Step-by-Step Blueprint: The Best Fibonacci Retracements Setup for Infosys

Key Takeaways

  • The 61.8% "Golden Ratio" is historically the most reliable reversal zone for Infosys during healthy market corrections.
  • Combining Fibonacci levels with the 200-day Exponential Moving Average (EMA) significantly filters out false breakout signals.
  • Always draw Fibonacci tools from the absolute swing low to the absolute swing high on daily and weekly charts for accurate long-term setups.

Why Infosys is the Perfect Candidate for Fibonacci Retracements in 2026

As we navigate the Indian equity markets in 2026, the technology sector—led by behemoths like Infosys (INFY)—continues to be a major driver of retail investor interest. With the global macroeconomic landscape stabilizing after years of interest rate adjustments and the rapid scaling of generative AI and Global Capability Centers (GCCs), Infosys exhibits clean, highly technical price action. For swing traders and long-term investors alike, this predictability makes it an ideal candidate for Fibonacci Retracement analysis.

Fibonacci Retracement is a popular technical analysis tool based on the key numbers identified by mathematician Leonardo Fibonacci. In the stock market, these ratios (23.6%, 38.2%, 50%, 61.8%, and 78.6%) act as potential support and resistance levels. Because institutional algorithmic trading systems and thousands of retail traders monitor these exact levels on blue-chip stocks like Infosys, these zones often become self-fulfilling prophecies, offering high-probability entry and exit setups.

The Step-by-Step Blueprint: Setting Up Your Fibonacci Tool on INFY

To successfully trade Infosys using Fibonacci Retracements, you must follow a structured, disciplined ruleset. Amateur traders often draw the tool arbitrarily, leading to poor entry signals. Here is the professional blueprint:

Step 1: Identify the Major Trend

Before plotting any lines, determine the dominant trend on the Daily (1D) or Weekly (1W) timeframe. Fibonacci retracements are trend-following tools; they do not work effectively in sideways or highly choppy markets. Look for a series of Higher Highs and Higher Lows for an uptrend, or Lower Lows and Lower Highs for a downtrend.

Step 2: Plot the Swing Low to Swing High

In an established uptrend, select the Fibonacci Retracement tool on your charting platform. Click on the absolute *Swing Low* (the lowest point of the recent trend start) and drag the cursor to the absolute *Swing High* (the peak of the trend before the current correction began). Ensure you include the candle wicks, not just the body, for precise calculations.

Step 3: Monitor the "Golden Zone" Confluence

Once plotted, pay close attention to the 50% and 61.8% retracement levels. In technical analysis, the region between 50% and 61.8% is considered the "Golden Zone" or the "Pocket." When Infosys pulls back to this area, do not blindly place a buy order. Instead, look for a confluence of indicators:

  • RSI (Relative Strength Index): Is the daily RSI approaching the oversold territory (below 30 or 40)?
  • Moving Averages: Is the 200-day EMA or 50-day EMA aligning with the 61.8% Fibonacci level?
  • Candlestick Patterns: Look for a bullish hammer, engulfing pattern, or morning star right at the Fibonacci level to confirm buyers are stepping back in.

Advanced Execution: Managing Risk and Placing Stop-Losses

No strategy is 100% accurate, and protecting your capital is the single most important job of a trader. When executing a long position on Infosys at the 61.8% retracement level, your stop-loss should logically be placed slightly below the next major Fibonacci level—the 78.6% retracement, or just below the swing low. This ensures that if the structural trend breaks, you exit with a minimal, controlled loss.

For your target levels, you can apply Fibonacci Extensions (specifically the 1.272 and 1.618 levels) to project where the next leg of the uptrend might face selling pressure. This gives you a mathematical, objective reward-to-risk ratio of at least 1:2 or 1:3 before you even enter the trade.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best timeframe to use Fibonacci Retracements for Infosys?

For swing trading and identifying major investment entries, the Daily (1D) and Weekly (1W) timeframes are the most reliable. Intra-day traders can use the 15-minute chart, but these setups carry higher market noise and lower probability of success compared to higher timeframes.

Can Fibonacci Retracements be used for short selling Infosys during a downtrend?

Yes. In a downtrend, you draw the Fibonacci tool from the Swing High to the Swing Low. The retracement levels (specifically 50% and 61.8%) will act as resistance zones where you can initiate short positions, targeting the previous swing lows.
